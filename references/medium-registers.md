# Medium registers

Use this reference before a substantial medium-specific draft or when adapting
the same message across formats.

Voice bends around the artifact's job. A family resemblance is useful; a shared
template is not.

## Outcome map

| Medium | Actual outcome | False success | Evidence of success |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Email | Reader understands the answer, decision, or next step and feels properly addressed. | Polished warmth buries the ask. | They can reply or act without rereading. |
| Letter | Relationship and truth feel present on the page. | Generic poetry or branded intimacy. | Details could only belong to these people. |
| Invoice or payment note | Amount, reason, due date, and payment route are unmistakable. | Charm softens or obscures the obligation. | No practical question remains. |
| Invitation | Guests feel wanted and know when, where, and how to respond. | A beautiful line hides logistics. | Who, what, when, where, RSVP, and access needs are clear. |
| Proposal or estimate | Scope, responsibility, fee, timing, exclusions, and decision are legible. | Persuasive fog or friendliness replaces terms. | Both sides can point to the same agreement. |
| Website or campaign | Visitor understands the offering, remembers the point, sees proof, and knows the next action. | Clever copy has no proposition or evidence. | The page still works without typography or motion. |
| Social post | A complete thought, update, or invitation rewards attention. | Engagement bait or faux intimacy. | It says something without begging for response. |
| Announcement | People understand what changed, why it matters, and what happens next. | Excitement replaces information. | Practical consequences are clear. |
| Handover | The next person can continue accurately. | A polished recap has no decisions, owners, or next actions. | Work can proceed without reconstructing context. |
| Apology | Responsibility, impact, and repair are clear. | Elegant self-explanation or wit acts as defence. | The harmed person is not asked to comfort the writer. |
| Sensitive, grief, or medical | Dignity, accuracy, and presence. | Warmth theatre or compulsory optimism. | The writing does not make the situation smaller. |
| Utility, error, legal, or consent | Reader knows what happened, what it means, what is optional, and what to do. | Brand voice interferes with recovery or rights. | State and next action are explicit. |

## Email

### Default

Direct, warm, organised.

Lead with the answer, update, or thing being sent. Put useful context after it.
Make the required decision or action easy to find.

```text
Subject
Answer or purpose
Useful context
Decision or next action
Warm close
```

### Voice ceiling

One small turn or human line is usually enough.

### Avoid

- reflexive “Hope this email finds you well”;
- long throat-clearing;
- bad news hidden below niceties;
- a clever sign-off after serious material;
- several asks trapped inside one paragraph.

## Letter

### Default

More room, more presence, fewer business structures.

Let a specific memory, observation, or honest feeling lead. Write for the person,
not an imagined audience who might admire the letter.

### Voice ceiling

Warmth and poetry may rise. Wit remains situational.

### Avoid

- generic tributes;
- fake profundity;
- a list of virtues standing in for relationship;
- studio branding inside private feeling.

## Invoice and payment communication

### Default

Calm, exact, unembarrassed.

State:

- what the invoice or reminder is for;
- amount and currency;
- due date;
- payment method or where it can be found;
- what to do if anything is wrong.

### Voice ceiling

Very low. Kindness appears through clarity and dignity.

### Avoid

- jokes about money;
- apologising for charging;
- passive aggression;
- vague “gentle reminders” with no due date;
- tiny-print terms;
- false urgency.

## Wedding or event invitation

### Default

Welcoming, specific, alive. Delight belongs to the people and occasion, not a
copywriting trick.

Preserve:

- names;
- occasion;
- date and time;
- venue and address;
- RSVP route and deadline;
- access, dress, travel, food, children, or accommodation information where
  relevant.

### Voice ceiling

Medium. One memorable line may set the temperature. Logistics remain literal.

### Avoid

- ad-campaign language;
- generic romance;
- coded logistics;
- making guests decode the event;
- humour that creates uncertainty about a real instruction.

## Website, product, programme, and campaign

### Default

Useful truth, exact turn, immediate explanation, nearby proof, legible action.
The visitor remains the protagonist.

A strong anchor may compress or turn meaning. The following line must resolve
the useful proposition rather than repeat the headline.

### Voice ceiling

Selective. Major anchors may delight. Navigation, forms, prices, consent,
eligibility, errors, and instructions stay literal.

### Avoid

- agency fog;
- claim inflation;
- every section using the same headline mechanism;
- typography asked to manufacture meaning;
- Apple cosplay;
- a call to action whose destination is unclear.

## Proposal and estimate

### Default

Clear enough to prevent future disagreement. Warm enough to feel collaborative.

Distinguish:

- objective and scope;
- roles and responsibilities;
- schedule and dependencies;
- fee, currency, taxes, and payment schedule;
- review rounds and approval;
- exclusions and change handling;
- ownership, licensing, credit, privacy, and handover;
- decision and next step.

### Voice ceiling

Low to medium. Section titles may carry charm. Terms should not.

## Social post

### Default

Say one worthwhile thing. Use a natural opening, give the information or
observation, then end.

### Avoid

- “big news” without news;
- “thoughts?” by reflex;
- LinkedIn mini-sermons;
- hashtags as personality;
- false urgency;
- borrowed vulnerability for engagement.

## Announcement

### Default

Name what changed. Explain why it matters. State when it takes effect and what
people need to do.

Positive emotion may follow the information. It cannot substitute for it.

## Internal brief, note, or handover

### Default

Decisions, evidence, owners, open questions, and next actions. Soul here means
respecting the next person's time.

Distinguish `planned`, `built`, `checked`, `accepted`, `sent`, and `published`.
Do not collapse an open gate into “done”.

### Avoid

- narrative without operational value;
- hidden uncertainty;
- decorative headings;
- missing owner or next step;
- status claims without evidence.

## Apology

### Default

1. Name what the writer did or failed to do.
2. Name the impact without demanding agreement.
3. Apologise plainly.
4. State repair already made or the concrete next step.
5. Explain only if explanation helps repair rather than self-defence.

Do not ask the harmed person to reassure, forgive, or manage the writer's guilt.

## Personal or sensitive writing

### Default

Plainness first. When the matter is painful, serious, or vulnerable, let the
human truth carry the writing.

No wit unless the relationship and moment clearly invite it.

Do not force optimism, compress grief into a lesson, or claim to understand an
experience the speaker cannot know.

## Forms, errors, legal, and consent

### Default

Literal, useful, calm.

The reader should know:

- what happened;
- what is required;
- what is optional;
- what they are agreeing to;
- what will happen to their information;
- how to decline, change, recover, or get help.

Brand personality must never obscure status, rights, money, privacy, or consent.
