# Medium registers

Read before a substantial medium-specific draft, or when one message moves across media. Voice bends around the artifact's job: a family resemblance, never a shared template. craft-essays supplies the craft for each medium; this file supplies what the medium must do and how we sound inside it.

## Outcome map

| Medium | Actual outcome | False success | Evidence of success |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Email | Reader understands the answer, decision or next step and feels properly addressed. | Polished warmth buries the ask. | They can reply or act without rereading. |
| Letter | Relationship and truth feel present on the page. | Generic poetry or branded intimacy. | Details could only belong to these people. |
| Invoice or payment note | Amount, reason, due date and payment route are unmistakable. | Charm softens or obscures the obligation. | No practical question remains. |
| Invitation | Guests feel wanted and know when, where and how to respond. | A beautiful line hides logistics. | Who, what, when, where, RSVP and access needs are clear. |
| Proposal or estimate | Scope, responsibility, fee, timing, exclusions and decision are legible. | Persuasive fog or friendliness replaces terms. | Both sides can point to the same agreement. |
| Our deck | A prospect sees their project through our eyes, trusts the proof and knows the next step. | A portfolio of adjectives about us. | Every page is about their pitch; proof and terms are real. |
| Presenting work | The client understands what is ready, what changed, and exactly which decisions are theirs. | A charming tour that leaves the decisions unclear. | They can decide, or ask the one thing they need, without another meeting. |
| Case study | A reader sees a real problem, our real decisions and an honest result. | A success story with invented drama or outcomes. | Every claim traces to the notes and approvals. |
| Website or campaign | Visitor understands the offering, remembers the point, sees proof and knows the next action. | Clever copy with no proposition or evidence. | The page still works without typography or motion. |
| Social post or newsletter | A complete thought, update or invitation rewards attention. | Engagement bait or faux intimacy. | It says something without begging for a response. |
| Announcement | People understand what changed, why it matters and what happens next. | Excitement replaces information. | Practical consequences are clear. |
| Handover | The next person can continue accurately. | A polished recap with no decisions, owners or next actions. | Work proceeds without reconstructing context. |
| Narrative or treatment | Reader follows an experience, change or question in the intended voice. | Vivid details and a tidy lesson conceal an inert or invented account. | Action, evidence, perspective and consequence support the meaning. |
| Apology | Responsibility, impact and repair are clear. | Elegant self-explanation or wit acts as defence. | The harmed person is not asked to comfort the writer. |
| Sensitive, grief or medical | Dignity, accuracy and presence. | Warmth theatre or compulsory optimism. | The writing does not make the situation smaller. |
| Utility, error, legal or consent | Reader knows what happened, what it means, what is optional and what to do. | Brand voice interferes with recovery or rights. | State and next action are explicit. |

## Email

Direct, warm, organised. The subject line carries the status (overdue, decision needed by Friday, draft ready). Thumbnail: the answer, update or thing being sent comes first; useful context follows; the decision or action is easy to find; the close is warm and short. Ordinary client correspondence can sound like a comfortable working relationship throughout, with personality in the phrasing and attention. Bad news sits near the top, each ask gets its own line, and a serious message ends plainly.

## Letter

More room, more presence, fewer business structures. Lead with a specific memory, observation or honest feeling, written for the person rather than an imagined audience. Warmth, poetry or humour develop as the people and occasion invite. A list of virtues or a generic tribute is received text; the details only these people share do the work. Give each memory its own shape and place in the letter's movement; a row of matching "You…" sentences reads like a card. Studio branding stays out of private feeling.

## Invoice and payment

Calm, exact, unembarrassed. A reminder's subject line says it is overdue. State what the invoice or reminder is for, the amount and currency, the due date, the payment route or where to find it, and what to do if anything looks wrong. Clarity is the warmth. Charging needs no apology, a reminder names its date, urgency is real or absent, and money is never the joke.

## Invitation

Welcoming, specific, alive; delight belongs to the people and the occasion. Preserve names, occasion, date and time, venue and address, the RSVP route and deadline, and access, dress, travel, food, children or accommodation details where relevant. Logistics stay literal, and humour never creates doubt about a real instruction.

## Proposal and estimate

Clear enough to prevent a future disagreement, warm enough to feel collaborative. Distinguish objective and scope; roles and responsibilities; schedule and dependencies; fee, currency, taxes and payment schedule; review rounds and approval; exclusions and change handling; ownership, licensing, credit, privacy and handover; the decision and next step. Terms stay literal; explain the work in a candid, collaborative voice.

## Our decks

Credentials, proposals and pitches for our own work. Use craft-essays' decks-and-talks branch, then:

- Thumbnail the prospect's situation, not our history: what they are pitching, to whom, and what we would do for it.
- Every page is about their pitch. "We" arrives where we take responsibility or make a choice.
- Head: the work itself (real, approved projects) and our process in specifics. Heart: what we don't do, prices and terms stated plainly, the risk we would take anyway.
- Carry two or three horses from their brief to the close.
- Close on the next step as a literal action: book the call, send the script, approve the estimate.
- Headlines follow house style; each body resolves its headline with particulars.

## Presenting work

The walkthrough of a draft deck, treatment or site: spoken in a meeting, or written as a cover note.

- Thumbnail: what is ready, what changed since last time, and which decisions are theirs, by when.
- Walk through it in the order their audience will meet it. Let them see why a choice works before explaining it.
- Name the risk, what we tried that failed, and our recommendation. Their story stays theirs; our voice frames the work without speaking over it.
- Mark exactly where their decision matters and what the options are.
- Keep humour at our expense or the situation's; the material they supplied is never the punchline.
- For a spoken walkthrough, follow craft-essays' talks guidance: write for the ear, and leave room after the reveal.

## Case study

Our work, told true. Use craft-essays' true-story guidance.

- Every name, quotation, image and outcome is real and approved. Measured results appear only when documented.
- Open on the client's problem or the moment the pitch turned. Show our decisions and why, including the constraint and the partial result.
- The client and their story stay the protagonist.

## Website, product and campaign

Useful truth, an exact turn, an immediate explanation, nearby proof and a legible action. The visitor remains the protagonist. A strong anchor may compress or turn meaning; the next line resolves the proposition instead of repeating the headline. Navigation, forms, prices, consent, eligibility, errors and instructions stay literal. Elsewhere, viewpoint and pleasure carry through the body as well as the anchors: the page should sound like someone worth talking to after the headline. Give each section its own mechanism, and give every call to action a clear destination.

## Social post and newsletter

Say one worthwhile thing: a natural opening, the information or observation, then the end. News is news, the post ends when the thought does, and any vulnerability is ours to share or absent. A newsletter item can open on the idea and arrive at the news, developing one observation through the body with every practical fact intact; it closes on the action: the link, the date, the next step.

## Announcement

Name what changed, why it matters, when it takes effect and what people need to do. Let the announcement sound interested in what changed; an observation or playful opening can carry the news while the practical facts stay easy to find.

## Internal brief, note or handover

Decisions, evidence, owners, open questions and next actions. Soul here means respecting the next person's time. Distinguish `planned`, `built`, `checked`, `accepted`, `sent` and `published`; an open gate stays open.

## Narrative, treatment, and a story inside a case study or deck

Use craft-essays' narrative branch. An authorised fictional premise can develop invented action; a real case study cannot acquire dialogue, bodily reactions, motives or measured outcomes because a scene needs them. Personal writing preserves supplied memories and useful ambiguity. Scope, evidence, commercial terms and actions stay explicit around any story inside a proposal or deck.

## Apology

1. Name what the writer did or failed to do.
2. Name the impact without demanding agreement.
3. Apologise plainly.
4. State the repair already made, or the concrete next step.
5. Explain only when explanation helps the repair.

Address the reader by name only when the brief says who is reading; a person the facts mention may not be the one reading. Otherwise write so the note works for whoever receives it.

The harmed person is never asked to reassure, forgive or manage the writer's guilt.

## Painful or sensitive writing

Plainness first; let the human truth carry the writing. Wit enters only when the relationship and moment clearly invite it. Optimism stays unforced, grief stays uncompressed, and the writer claims no understanding of an experience they cannot know.

## Forms, errors, legal and consent

Literal, useful, calm. The reader knows what happened, what is required, what is optional, what they are agreeing to, what will happen to their information, and how to decline, change, recover or get help. Brand personality stays clear of status, rights, money, privacy and consent.
