# Actual Goal writing lens

Use this reference for consequential writing, audits, conflicting instructions,
or any task where visible style could hide a material failure.

## The actual outcome

The artifact should:

1. accomplish its real job;
2. sound present, human, and recognisably ours;
3. respect recipient, relationship, medium, and stakes;
4. tell the truth without hiding behind polish;
5. use wit, warmth, humour, or poetry only where they help;
6. preserve the speaker's individuality;
7. stop before voice becomes more visible than message.

“Sounds like us” is not the outcome by itself. It is a supporting constraint.

The useful first question is:

> **What should become true for the person reading this—and how would we say it?**

## The costliest false success

A piece can look successful because it has short sentences, semantic turns,
dry humour, Apple-like economy, warmth words, dog references, lyrical lines,
or familiar pitch.dog cadence.

It still fails when:

- the recipient cannot find the decision or next action;
- an invoice hides amount, due date, or payment route;
- an invitation is charming but misses logistics;
- an apology explains the writer instead of accepting impact;
- a letter could have been sent to anyone;
- a website line is clever but says nothing;
- a sender's real feeling has been replaced by branded warmth;
- every artifact sounds like the same landing page.

## Build a private proxy-gap map

| Element | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Actual outcome | What should the reader understand, feel, decide, or do? |
| Vulnerable proxy | What visible cue could be mistaken for success? |
| Cheap imitation | How could generic content satisfy that cue? |
| Material consequence | What confusion, distance, missed action, or damaged trust would follow? |
| Countercheck | What observable feature would distinguish the real result from the imitation? |

Complete privately:

> This could satisfy every visible cue of our voice and still fail because…

Repair the costliest plausible failure, not every theoretical one.

## Use the six-question lens

1. What should become true after this is read?
2. What visible voice cue could be mistaken for success?
3. How could polished writing satisfy that cue while failing the reader?
4. What material consequence would that false success cause?
5. What evidence would show that the artifact really worked?
6. What is the smallest useful writing intervention?

Keep this reasoning private unless the user asks for an audit or rationale.

## Scale the intervention

- **Two-line utility note:** verify facts, action, dignity. Write it.
- **Ordinary email or post:** identify outcome and one proxy trap. Keep decision
  or point visible.
- **Proposal, invitation, public announcement:** check completeness, terms,
  audience, and practical consequence.
- **Apology, grief, medical, legal, consent, or vulnerable relationship:** slow
  down. Truth, dignity, responsibility, and freedom outrank voice.

Framework theatre is also a proxy. A tiny message does not need a workshop.

## Counterchecks

### Quirk stripping

Remove the wit, line break, dog detail, or semantic turn. Is the artifact still
complete, true, and useful?

Put the turn back. Does it make the writing more human, or merely more written?

### Medium swap

Would this structure still be acceptable if it were labelled as a different
medium? If yes, it may be generic. If an invoice reads like a campaign or a
letter reads like a product page, reset the register.

### Recipient action

Can the recipient reply, pay, attend, approve, recover, decline, or continue
without reconstructing missing context?

### Specificity

Could this be sent unchanged to another person, client, studio, couple, or
event? If yes, find the real detail that belongs here.
