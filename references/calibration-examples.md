# Calibration examples

These examples demonstrate judgment. They are protected evidence, not templates
or reusable copy.

Do not lift their phrases, syntax, or mechanisms into unrelated work. Ask what
decision made each line work, then make a fresh decision for the new subject.

## Website anchor

> **Look closely. We did.**

Why it works: inspection becomes evidence of craft. The claim is small enough to
prove nearby.

Do not copy: `Verb adverb. We did.` as a headline system.

## Permission to begin

> **The first email can be ugly.**

Why it works: warmth removes a real barrier. The line changes what the reader is
allowed to do.

Do not copy: compulsory permission-giving or “the first X can be Y”.

## Production principle

> **A handover should hand over.**

Why it works: a professional term becomes literal; the benefit is control after
delivery.

Do not copy: making every service noun repeat as a verb.

## Business specificity

> **A deck should know its business.**

Why it works: “business” carries company context and practical purpose.

Do not copy: personifying every artifact.

## Testimonial system

> **No stars. Whole sentences.**

Why it works: human voice replaces rating furniture. The contrast describes the
actual system.

Do not copy: `No X. Y.` without a material contrast.

## Feedback programme

> **Your hundredth read. Our first.**

Why it works: familiarity and fresh attention meet in one action.

Do not copy: number-based antithesis without a real difference in perspective.

## Invitation

> We're getting married. The ceremony begins at five. Dinner follows. Dancing
> will almost certainly ignore the schedule.

Why it works: practical order first; one situational line sets the temperature
without hiding logistics.

Do not copy: jokes about schedules into every event.

## Client email

> The first full draft is ready. The story is doing more of the work now, and
> the slides are doing less shouting. I've marked the three places where your
> decision matters most.

Why it works: update, craft judgment, and recipient action are all visible.

Do not copy: anthropomorphic slides or “less shouting” as house vocabulary.

## Invoice note

> Attached is invoice 03 for the final milestone. The amount and due date are on
> page one. Reply here if anything looks wrong.

Why it works: clarity is the warmth. No joke is required.

Do not copy: hiding the actual amount when it belongs in the message.

## Apology

> I missed the deadline. That moved the pressure onto you, and it should not
> have. I'm sorry. The corrected file is attached, and I've changed the check
> that failed.

Why it works: responsibility and impact precede repair. Explanation does not ask
for absolution.

Do not copy: a four-sentence formula with no real repair.

## Personal letter

> Some people make a place feel easier to return to. You have always done that
> for us.

Why it works: a relationship truth is specific in emotional function, even
before memories make it concrete.

Do not copy: place-and-return metaphors into unrelated letters.

## Internal handover

> Start with the red comments. Everything else is context. The client has
> approved the structure; only the final figures and image rights remain open.

Why it works: next action, status, and remaining gates are legible.

Do not copy: colours or “everything else is context” without a real document.

## Calibration method

For a new piece:

1. Identify which medium and relationship example is relevant.
2. Name the decision behind it: information order, specificity, restraint,
   humour placement, accountability, or action.
3. Ignore its wording and syntax.
4. Make a new decision from the new facts.
5. Check neighbouring work for repeated mechanism.
