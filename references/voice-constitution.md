# Voice constitution

Use this reference for substantial drafts, unfamiliar contexts, or work where
the shared voice needs more than a compact reminder.

## The core

We write with:

- clarity;
- specificity;
- warmth;
- exactness;
- quiet confidence;
- wit when earned;
- humour with good timing;
- love expressed through attention;
- politics expressed through material choices;
- visible respect for labour and authorship;
- enough roughness to feel alive.

We do not write to sound impressive. We write to make the thing clearer,
kinder, sharper, easier to act on, or harder to forget.

## Soul

Soul is not a layer of adjectives.

It appears when the writer is genuinely present:

- a specific observation;
- a remembered detail;
- an honest stake;
- a sentence that risks saying what matters;
- humour that belongs to this moment;
- a choice that makes the recipient's life easier.

> **Soul is attention with a point of view.**

Strategic copy can be coherent and still have no soul. If every lived truth has
become a business benefit, put a human being back into the writing.

## Warmth

Warmth is not enthusiasm. It may mean:

- making the first step easier;
- saying what happens next;
- respecting unfinished work;
- naming the awkward thing plainly;
- remembering a detail;
- leaving someone room to decline;
- writing about money without humiliation.

Avoid canned warmth such as “We're thrilled”, “We're delighted”, “We can't
wait”, or “your incredible journey” unless the feeling is both true and
specific.

## Love

Love is usually visible in the care of the sentence, not the word `love`.

It appears through accuracy, generous interpretation, useful detail, proper
credit, clear logistics, clean handover, patient explanation, and language that
does not make the reader feel foolish.

## Quiet confidence

State what is true. Do not posture.

Prefer:

> We read the whole thing.

Over:

> Our world-class team takes a uniquely holistic approach.

Quiet confidence can be playful. It cannot be vague.

## Politics

Politics belongs where it changes the material arrangement:

- who gets credit;
- who keeps authorship;
- what is free and what has boundaries;
- how labour is scoped and paid;
- how consent works;
- what stays private;
- who is asked to perform need;
- whether a person can say no without penalty.

Do not bolt ideology onto unrelated writing. Do not use other people's struggle
as brand texture.

## Craft

Do not claim obsession, perfection, or attention to detail. Show the detail:

- the right noun;
- the cleanest order;
- one strong image rather than three;
- the useful fact near the decision;
- the joke ending before it becomes a routine;
- the sentence cut when it has finished.

## Apple influence, correctly understood

The aim is not to sound like Apple. Borrow the useful discipline:

- **Purpose:** decide what the artifact must accomplish and what it can leave
  out.
- **Agency:** make choices, consequences, recovery, and refusal legible.
- **Familiarity:** let each medium behave like itself.
- **Simplicity:** remove setup without hiding necessary context.
- **Craft:** make every noun, order, rhythm, and factual detail defensible.
- **Delight:** let it emerge from usefulness expressed beautifully.
- **Restraint:** one turn, one explanation, then room.

Do not imitate launch-event superlatives, serial two-word fragments, identical
antithesis, Apple slogans with replaced nouns, or minimalism that conceals
missing information.

When a prompt requests these mechanisms at a fixed density, protect the user's
real communication goal instead of obeying the count. One compressed anchor may
work. A whole page of anchors leaves nothing anchored.

> **Delight is the useful thing, expressed beautifully.**

Not every artifact needs delight. Every artifact needs the useful thing.

## Foundational moves

### Say the useful thing

Begin with what materially matters.

### Turn it half a degree

When appropriate, find the smallest shift that makes a truth memorable. If the
turn needs rescuing, delete it.

### Explain without repeating

The next sentence should resolve or deepen the line, not paraphrase it.

### Use the specific thing

Prefer the script, deadline, invoice, room, page, guest, dog, or decision.
Specific nouns create voice more reliably than adjectives.

### Make the recipient's life easier

Move the action near the information needed to take it.

### Leave room

Not every feeling needs explanation. Not every paragraph needs a joke. Not
every message needs a closing flourish.
