# *Small Hours*, rebuilt around the bakery.

Coralie Dunn came to us two weeks before her first financier meeting. She's a first-time director, and *Small Hours*, her dark comedy feature, is set in a night-shift bakery. She was pitching it to financiers with a 62-page deck.

A first-time director is asking financiers to back a feature she hasn't made yet, which puts a lot of weight on the deck. What Coralie did have was a setting, a tone, a short film and the film in her head. None of it needed inventing. The job was getting it onto the page.

We had two weeks, which leaves room for a few big decisions rather than a long list of small ones. The first was length. A 62-page deck holds a lot of thinking, more than a first meeting with a financier has room for. We cut it to 24 pages.

Then we rebuilt the story section around the bakery. The small hours are when a night-shift bakery does its work, so the title and the setting were already pointing at the same place. Now the story section points there too.

The stock images went as well. Stock photography could illustrate anybody's film, so in its place are frames from Coralie's short film. They show financiers her eye, and something she has already made.

Coralie puts it better than we can. "It finally looks like the film in my head, only with fewer pages."

A deck can't promise financing, so here is where things stand. Coralie has had four financier meetings so far. One financier asked for the full script, which is the next step a deck exists to earn. No financing is confirmed.
