# *Small Hours*, in 24 pages

Coralie Dunn is a first-time director, and *Small Hours* is her dark comedy feature. She came to us two weeks before her first financier meeting, with a 62-page deck that left nothing about the film out.

That's a good thing to have and a hard thing to pitch from. A deck that long usually means someone cares about a film and hasn't yet had to choose what goes first. Cutting is mostly that choice: what a financier needs to see in the first meeting, and what can wait for a later conversation. With two weeks to go, the choosing had to start straight away.

We cut the deck to 24 pages, which is plenty of room for a whole film when every page has a job. We rebuilt the story section around the night-shift bakery where the film is set, so the story has somewhere to stand before it asks anyone to laugh. Then we replaced the stock images with frames from Coralie's short film. Stock photography can suggest a mood, but it can't tell a financier anything about the person asking for the money. Frames from a director's own work show what she does with a camera, and when you're a first-time director, that is worth more than any mood.

The bakery, the frames and the film were already hers. Our part was the choosing, the order and the design.

> "It finally looks like the film in my head, only with fewer pages."
> — Coralie Dunn

Since then, Coralie has had four financier meetings, and one financier has asked for the full script. No financing is confirmed.

That's where things stand. We'd rather tell you that plainly than round it up into a happier ending.
