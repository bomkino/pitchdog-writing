The main issue is the repeated mechanism: **all ten headings use two short beats separated by a full stop.** Contrast, repetition, pronoun pairing and progression vary underneath, but the reading rhythm barely changes.

These headings should help visitors understand the offer and distinguish sections. The costliest false success is a page that sounds consistently polished while giving readers few clues about where to find what they need.

| Heading | Assessment |
|---|---|
| ‘Big stories. Small screens.’ | An intelligible contrast between narrative scale and screen size. “Small screens” suggests TV or mobile; that distinction needs to belong to the actual offer. |
| ‘Real people. Real stakes.’ | Suggests factual storytelling. Repeating “real” supplies emphasis, but leaves the people and consequences unspecified. |
| ‘Sharp thinking. Soft landing.’ | The sound pairing is more precise than the meaning. “Soft landing” could mean an easy process, safe creative choices or a smooth delivery. |
| ‘No fluff. Just film.’ | “Film” gives a category cue. “No fluff” is a familiar claim of efficiency without a defined working practice behind it. |
| ‘Your vision. Our craft.’ | Names the client–maker relationship in interchangeable agency language. Gives no detail about authorship, collaboration or responsibility. |
| ‘More feeling. Less fuss.’ | Promises emotional impact and ease. Both need support nearby; the comparative structure leaves the baseline unstated. |
| ‘One team. Every frame.’ | Contains a potentially useful promise of continuity. Its strength depends on what the team actually handles and whether that involvement lasts throughout production. |
| ‘Good work. Better together.’ | Offers praise and a collaboration sentiment. The least informative heading: it could introduce almost any section in almost any business. |
| ‘From first thought. To final cut.’ | Contains useful process boundaries, suggesting involvement from conception through editing. The full stop splits a continuous “from…to…” construction to preserve the house rhythm. |
| ‘Made here. Seen everywhere.’ | Connects production location with audience reach. “Here” needs a clear referent; “everywhere” risks overstating distribution. |

The material worth preserving is the screen-size distinction, team continuity and process scope—where those reflect the business. They give the writing something concrete to develop.

The surrounding copy is unavailable, so missing explanation or evidence cannot be treated as a confirmed page-level failure. Headings need useful support nearby; they need not contain the entire offer themselves.

Prioritise these repairs:

1. Assign each heading a distinct information job tied to its section.
2. Retain the paired cadence selectively; vary the remaining structures according to their jobs.
3. Address the least informative language first, particularly “Good work,” “Your vision” and “Soft landing.”
4. Check the implied claims about continuity, ease, scope and reach against the actual service and evidence.
