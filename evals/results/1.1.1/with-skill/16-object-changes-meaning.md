On the community print shop’s last afternoon, Mara wrapped a brass composing stick in her handkerchief and pocketed it. She wanted to keep the ink in its corners.

At the next bench, a girl was pushing loose type against a ruler. She held one end down with her wrist. When she reached for another letter, the ruler shifted and two pieces fell flat.

Mara took her hand off the door handle.

“Wait.”

She unwrapped the stick and put it in the girl’s palm.

“You can keep this.”

The girl lifted it a little, then made room on the bench. Mara loosened the screw and adjusted the width. Together they stood the fallen letters in the stick, working from the pencilled line on a scrap of paper. The girl fitted the last letter. Mara moved her thumb so the girl could put hers there.
