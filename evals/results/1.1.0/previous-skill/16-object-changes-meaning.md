On the community print shop’s last afternoon, Leena wrapped a brass composing stick in a scrap of its letterhead and slipped it into her pocket. She pressed the bundle flat so it wouldn’t catch on her keys.

At the door she stopped. A girl at the remaining table was nudging loose type against a ruler. Whenever she reached for another letter, the end one tipped. She stood it up again.

Leena took the bundle out.

“Keep this.”

She unwrapped the stick, put it in the girl’s palm and pulled a stool alongside hers. Leena steadied it while the girl transferred the letters, then helped fill the gap at the end of their first line. The last space wouldn’t fit. They tried a thinner one.

“Try lifting your thumb.”

The girl did. Nothing fell. With her free hand, she slid the ruler aside.
