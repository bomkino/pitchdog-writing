**samey-syntax-audit — All ten headings use the same two-beat mechanism.** The repeated full stops, contrasts and balanced fragments make the next line predictable.

**Actual outcome:** Help visitors understand the film offering and distinguish what each section contains.

**Costliest false success:** The page looks concise and consistent, but visitors remember the cadence without understanding the service or why this team fits their project.

**Already alive:** “Screens”, “film”, “frame” and “final cut” give the set a subject. The journey from an initial thought to an edit offers a useful process structure. Brevity helps scanning; the problem is how little changes between headings.

| Heading | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| “Big stories. Small screens.” | A readable scale contrast. Potentially useful if small-screen work is the actual specialism; otherwise it misdirects. |
| “Real people. Real stakes.” | Repeated “Real” asserts authenticity without identifying the people or what they stand to lose. |

**Material gaps:** Section copy and supporting evidence weren’t supplied. These headings alone cannot establish service scope, production responsibility or distribution reach. That limits the audit; it does not establish that the page lacks proof.

**High-impact repairs, in order:**

1. Assign each section a distinct information job before adjusting cadence.
2. Resolve “small screens”, “soft landing”, “here” and “everywhere” against the actual offering and evidence.
3. Challenge the need for “Your vision. Our craft.” and “Good work. Better together.” if both introduce collaboration without adding different information.
4. Reserve the paired-fragment form for the strongest anchor or two. Let the remaining structures follow their content.
5. Check the headings alone in page order: visitors should be able to anticipate each section without repeatedly encountering another general promise.
