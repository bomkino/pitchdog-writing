## Actual outcome

The headings must distinguish ten sections, build a clear page narrative, and communicate what pitch.dog makes, how it works, and why that matters.

## Costliest false success

They look concise, cinematic, and confident while saying very little. All ten use the same two-beat, full-stop mechanism, turning distinct sections into one repeated brand gesture.

## Already alive

- Plain language; little agency jargon.
- “Real people. Real stakes.” connects human subjects with consequence.
- “One team. Every frame.” could express an integrated production model.
- “From first thought. To final cut.” signals end-to-end involvement.
- “Made here. Seen everywhere.” contains a useful tension between place and reach.
- Story, people, thinking, craft, feeling, team, process, and distribution form a potentially strong content map.

## Generic or performative

| Heading | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| “Big stories. Small screens.” | Familiar scale contrast. “Small screens” is ambiguous and may diminish the work rather than locate it. |
| “Real people. Real stakes.” | Strongest line, but still broadly available to documentaries, charities, journalism, and reality television. “Real” needs contextual truth. |
| “Sharp thinking. Soft landing.” | Neat alliteration disguises an unclear proposition. “Soft landing” suggests crisis management or economics more readily than filmmaking. |
| “No fluff. Just film.” | Negative positioning without substance. Unless the section proves what gets removed, the anti-fluff line becomes fluff itself. |
| “Your vision. Our craft.” | Stock agency formula. It reduces the client to vision and pitch.dog to execution, flattening shared authorship. |
| “More feeling. Less fuss.” | Textbook antithesis. Both nouns are abstract, and “less fuss” makes an unsupported ease claim about difficult production work. |
| “One team. Every frame.” | Totalising claim. It may obscure collaborators, freelancers, specialists, or divided responsibilities. |
| “Good work. Better together.” | A pleasant truism with no distinguishing information. “Better” has no stated comparison or evidence. |
| “From first thought. To final cut.” | Common production-span claim. The full stop interrupts the grammatical `from…to…` construction for surface drama. |
| “Made here. Seen everywhere.” | Attractive cadence, vague substance. “Here” is undefined; “everywhere” is an absolute reach claim. |

## Material gaps

- No heading identifies a specific service, format, audience, method, decision, or result.
- The claims “real,” “one team,” “every frame,” and “seen everywhere” need evidence nearby.
- The headings do not reveal why each section exists or how the page progresses.
- “Here,” “small screens,” “soft landing,” “good work,” and “less fuss” lack stable meanings without body copy.
- The suite does not distinguish pitch.dog from another competent production company.
- Section context is missing, so alignment between each heading and its actual content cannot be verified.

## High-impact repairs

1. Keep the `X. Y.` construction for no more than one or two headings where the contrast carries real meaning.
2. Give every section one distinct job: proposition, process, proof, people, terms, work, or action.
3. Anchor headings in specific nouns and consequences drawn from the section beneath them.
4. Verify every totalising or comparative claim; qualify or remove anything the page cannot prove.
5. Vary syntax, length, and temperature so the sequence develops rather than resets ten times.
6. Preserve the useful content map—story, people, thinking, craft, team, process, reach—but replace slogan symmetry with actual information.
7. Read the headings consecutively without body copy. If the next construction is predictable, the mechanism is still louder than the meaning.
