One monsoon afternoon, a mother and daughter clear the old flat after the grandmother’s death. They work slowly, making small decisions around a larger absence. Clothes are folded. Cupboards are emptied one by one. Rain closes the windows and darkens the rooms. Then they find twelve undeveloped rolls of film. They count them twice and say nothing. The afternoon continues: water, cardboard, dust, breath. Between them, the rolls hold a life neither woman knew had been kept. The flat is nearly empty. What remains cannot be carried down the stairs.
