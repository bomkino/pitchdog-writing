Mara slipped the brass composing stick into her coat pocket. A keepsake, she’d decided, though it pulled her coat crooked. At the door of the community print shop, she stopped to read the closing notice again.

A scrape came from the bench. Another girl had laid a ruler beside a row of loose type. Each time she straightened one end, a letter at the other end tipped over.

Mara put her bag down.

“Here. You can keep this.”

She drew out the stick, picking a thread from its screw, and placed it in the girl’s palm. Together they set its width. Mara passed her the letters one by one, then showed her where to rest her thumb while they fitted the spaces.

The girl eased her thumb away. The first line stayed upright.
